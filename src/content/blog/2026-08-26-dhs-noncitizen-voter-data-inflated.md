The Department of Homeland Security [acknowledged in a recorded call with Nevada election officials](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/25/nx-s1-5940807/noncitizen-voters-trump-nevada-call) earlier this month that the figure of nearly 16,000 noncitizens it claimed to have found on the state's voter rolls was "the ceiling" — a preliminary estimate requiring manual review for every entry — and subsequently provided the state with a list of just 185 voters it was "confident" were noncitizens. Nevada's deputy secretary of state for elections, Mark Wlaschin, told the federal officials that the state could not act on the information without identifying details. DHS declined to supply those details. NPR, which acquired records and a recording of the call via a public records request, confirmed that Pennsylvania had a similar call with DHS and also never received a verified smaller list. In July, President Trump had [told the country in a primetime address](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/25/nx-s1-5940807/noncitizen-voters-trump-nevada-call) that his administration had found more than 250,000 noncitizens on the voter rolls of just four states. More than a month later, the states are still asking for the underlying data. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin had threatened to hold local election officials criminally accountable if they did not act on the information; federal officials told Nevada officials no criminal referrals had actually been made.

## The received wisdom

The left-wing reading of this story is straightforward and, in this instance, substantially supported by the facts as reported: the administration made dramatic public claims to justify pre-election voter-roll purges, the claims appear not to have been supported by verified data, and the effect — if not the purpose — would be to pressure state officials into removing voters from rolls without adequate verification. California's statement to NPR captures this framing: "The real threat is that eligible voters — your friends, family, and neighbors — are recklessly and mistakenly removed from the voter rolls as a result of the administration's fishing expedition." Nevada's own election officials, hardly a progressive stronghold, explicitly said they couldn't act without "real verifiable information." The risk of eligible citizens being incorrectly purged is real and, in a midterm cycle where margins are narrow, consequential.

## A different read

The case for taking noncitizen voting seriously as a policy concern does not depend on the specific numbers DHS floated — and that's precisely where the administration's handling of this issue has done the most damage to its own argument. Voter registration systems in the United States vary enormously by state. The National Voter Registration Act requires states to make reasonable efforts to maintain accurate rolls, but the mechanisms for identifying noncitizens are imperfect: many rely on the honour system at the point of registration, and data-sharing between federal immigration agencies and state election databases has been patchy for years. A serious, methodologically rigorous audit of noncitizen registrations — conducted transparently and with adequate due-process protections — is entirely defensible policy. It is the kind of thing that sensible election administrators in democracies everywhere do routinely.

What DHS appears to have done instead is the opposite of that. According to the NPR account, the agency matched names, dates of birth and addresses from publicly available voter files against USCIS immigration records — a methodology that is well-known to produce large numbers of false positives. Names are not unique identifiers; addresses change; date-of-birth matching across databases of different quality generates substantial error rates. The 16,000 figure DHS announced for Nevada was, by the agency's own subsequent admission, "the ceiling" — the maximum possible number before any individual review. The 185 figure it provided with moderate confidence is roughly 1.2% of that ceiling. A sober reading of those numbers is not that 185 noncitizens are on Nevada's voter rolls — it is that DHS's methodology is producing results it cannot defend in a conversation with state officials, let alone in court.

The political incentive structure here is familiar. Administration officials gain a news cycle by announcing large, alarming numbers. Those numbers generate pressure on state officials and provide rhetorical ammunition for executive orders — like the mail-voting restrictions the Supreme Court [allowed to proceed earlier this week](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/25/first-thing-us-supreme-court-midterms-mail-in-voting-trump-plan) — that then develop their own legal and institutional momentum. By the time the actual data is scrutinised, the policy has already moved. Secretary Mullin's threat to prosecute local election officials who don't act on unverified data is the most troubling element: it turns the evidence problem inside out, using the claim as a coercive instrument before it has been validated. That is the opposite of how rule-of-law governance is supposed to work.

Conservatives who genuinely care about election integrity — and there is a legitimate tradition of that concern, running from the Carter-Baker commission recommendations of 2005 all the way through subsequent academic work on registration accuracy — should be troubled by this pattern. Inflated claims that later collapse do more damage to the credibility of election security as a cause than the opponents of voter-roll maintenance ever could. The administration has handed critics a perfect story: dramatic numbers, a presidential primetime address, and then a backroom acknowledgment that the data doesn't hold. If you want to make the case that noncitizen voting is a systematic problem, you need evidence that can survive scrutiny. This evidence did not.

## What to watch

- **State-level responses**: Whether Nevada, Pennsylvania, California or any other state acts on the DHS lists — or refuses to — will determine whether this becomes a legal confrontation before Election Day.
- **BIS criminal referrals**: Mullin threatened prosecution of state officials. Whether DHS actually makes any criminal referrals against state election administrators will reveal how far the administration will push this beyond rhetoric.
- **Ohio voter-ID ruling**: A separate federal judge has [blocked Ohio's new proof-of-citizenship voter registration law](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/25/us-judge-blocks-ohio-law-requiring-proof-of-citizenship-to-register-to-vote), on constitutional grounds; how that appeal proceeds will establish the outer legal limits of state-level citizenship verification requirements.
- **DHS's "additional 40 staff" review**: The agency told Nevada it was adding 40 staff to manually review voter records. Whether those reviews produce a credible, documentable list in time to affect any state's roll maintenance will be the empirical test of whether the program has any real validity.
