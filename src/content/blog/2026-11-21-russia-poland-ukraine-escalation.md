A Russian missile struck Polish territory on Friday during a NATO military exercise, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk confirmed, [according to BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqx2x9dklxnro). The strike occurred as Ukraine simultaneously [attacked the Kerch Bridge](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/20/ukraine-strikes-kerch-bridge-as-putin-ally-declares-no-one-needs), the critical logistical link between Russia and occupied Crimea, with a long-range strike drone. Russia responded by [targeting Kyiv's river crossings](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6x29gn4pdn9o) in a wave of missile attacks on the capital. Denmark separately announced it would expel a Russian defence attaché over an alleged [Moscow-directed plot to assassinate a US-Ukrainian rapper](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmde1lj4yjxno) before a concert in Copenhagen. In a further development, [the UK announced it would provide Ukraine with 1,000 long-range strike drones](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5n4em9mkkzeo) as part of the defence cooperation attached to the new UK-EU reset agreement. The Kremlin, through a senior ally, declared that "no one needs Europe" in response to the continent's declared readiness for peace talks. European leaders, meanwhile, [formally stated they were ready for negotiations with Russia to end the war](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckzd9nln5e6go).

## The received wisdom

The conventional Western framing is that Russia's escalatory pattern — striking NATO territory, targeting civilian infrastructure, running assassination plots in allied capitals — represents the behaviour of a regime that cannot be appeased and must be deterred through sustained military support for Ukraine. The provision of long-range weapons, in this view, is a rational response to Russia's demonstrated willingness to use force across borders. The Kerch Bridge strike is a legitimate military operation against a critical supply line. The Danish expulsion is a proportionate diplomatic response to a confirmed intelligence operation. The escalation, in the mainstream reading, originates entirely with Moscow, and the Western response has consistently been reactive and restrained. To do otherwise — to withhold weapons or pursue premature diplomacy — would be to reward aggression and invite more of it.

## A different read

The escalatory logic of this conflict has now crossed into territory that deserves more sober analysis than the headlines convey.

A Russian missile landing on Polish soil — a NATO member — during a military alliance exercise is not, categorically, a minor incident. The North Atlantic Treaty's Article 5 mutual defence commitment was designed precisely for moments like this. The fact that it has not been invoked, that Western governments are instead treating this as a localised incident requiring a measured response, tells us something important: NATO's Article 5 red line has been implicitly redefined in real time. The alliance decided, in this instance, that the costs of formally triggering a collective defence response outweighed the credibility damage of not doing so. That is a reasonable political calculation. It is also a precedent that adversaries observe.

The Kerch Bridge strike, [reported by Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/20/ukraine-strikes-kerch-bridge-as-putin-ally-declares-no-one-needs), complicates the picture further. Ukraine is using Western-supplied long-range weapons to strike targets deep inside Russian-controlled territory — the bridge is a vital supply corridor for Russian forces in Crimea. The military logic is impeccable. The strategic logic is more contested. Each Ukrainian strike on a high-profile Russian asset produces a cycle of Russian retaliation against Ukrainian civilian infrastructure and, increasingly, against NATO-adjacent targets. The question that Western governments have consistently avoided answering is what the endpoint of this escalatory cycle is, and whether the available offramps are being foreclosed faster than new ones are being opened.

The [UK drone commitment](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5n4em9mkkzeo) packaged into the EU reset deal is instructive. It binds British defence support to Ukraine into a broader diplomatic package, blurring the lines between trade policy and military commitment in ways that future governments will find constraining. The pattern of Western support — consistently sufficient to prevent Ukrainian defeat but historically insufficient to produce Ukrainian victory — has now stretched into its fourth year with no clear theory of decisive outcome. That is not an argument for abandoning Ukraine; it is an argument for being honest about what the goal actually is.

The Danish assassination plot, meanwhile, is a reminder that Russian intelligence operations against Western soil have continued without pause or effective deterrence through the entire war period. [BBC News reported](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmde1lj4yjxno) that Denmark is expelling the defence attaché — a standard diplomatic response — but the larger pattern of Russian asymmetric operations in Europe has not been successfully disrupted by diplomatic expulsions. The right-of-centre case here is for more robust counterintelligence action and, critically, for European governments to be honest with their populations about the degree to which Russian intelligence is operating inside their borders.

## What to watch

Watch NATO's formal response to the Polish missile strike: if the alliance issues only a statement of concern without any change in deterrence posture, it will confirm that Article 5's credibility has been quietly hollowed out for the specific case of Russian conventional missiles in ambiguous circumstances. Watch whether Ukraine's Kerch Bridge strike produces Russian retaliation beyond Ukraine's borders — if Moscow targets infrastructure in a NATO state in direct response, the calculus changes entirely. Watch the European peace talks proposal: if it proceeds as a European-only process without US backing following the APEC summit, it represents a significant fracture in Western cohesion. And watch the UK drone deployment timeline — if British weapons are confirmed to have been used in Russian territory within weeks of the commitment, the domestic political pressure on Burnham's government will intensify sharply.
