A US Senate committee voted on Thursday along party lines to hold former National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases director Anthony Fauci in contempt of Congress, clearing the way for a referral to the Justice Department for potential prosecution. The vote followed a 29 July hearing at which [Fauci invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination more than 100 times](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c1m18158my9o) in response to questions from committee chairman Rand Paul about his handling of the Covid-19 pandemic and the funding of research at the Wuhan Institute of Virology. Fauci holds a presidential pardon granted by Joe Biden on his last day in office in January 2025, which covers his actions between 2014 and 2025. However, the pardon does not cover contempt of Congress occurring after that date, nor state or local charges. Rand Paul bypassed a full Senate vote on the contempt resolution, citing a desire not to "waste time," though a full Senate vote would typically require a 60-vote supermajority. Whether the Justice Department will pursue the case remains uncertain.

## The received wisdom

Fauci's defenders make arguments that deserve to be taken at face value. David Schertler, his attorney, is correct that taking the Fifth Amendment is not an admission of guilt — it is a constitutional right designed precisely to protect individuals from compelled self-incrimination, regardless of whether they have done anything wrong. Rand Paul has for years, publicly and repeatedly, stated his desire to see Fauci "behind bars." That is not the posture of a neutral investigator seeking truth; it is the posture of a prosecutor who has decided the verdict before examining the evidence. The party-line vote, Paul's procedural bypass of full Senate deliberation, and the prosecutorial tone of the hearings all support the characterisation — offered by Fauci's legal team — that this is "a crude political stunt." Contempt prosecutions for Fifth Amendment assertion are legally untested territory, and most lawyers believe Fauci's legal ground is solid.

## A different read

All of that is true, and none of it is the end of the story.

The Biden pardon was itself an extraordinary act. Pre-emptive pardons — granted not in response to charges but in anticipation of potential future investigations — are constitutionally permissible but politically significant. Biden pardoned Fauci, his son, James Comey, and other figures on his final day in office. Whatever one thinks of the targets, the pattern of pre-emptive pardons by outgoing presidents is a bipartisan escalation cycle: it tells incoming administrations that their predecessors expect to be investigated, and that they need legal protection from it. That is not a sign of institutional health.

The substantive questions Rand Paul has been asking — about the origins of SARS-CoV-2 and the NIH's relationship with EcoHealth Alliance's Wuhan research — are not frivolous. The FBI and the Department of Energy have both previously concluded that a lab-related incident is the most likely origin of the pandemic, though with varying degrees of confidence and caveats. [Most scientists still favour a zoonotic origin](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c1m18158my9o), as the BBC notes. The question remains genuinely unresolved. The NIH's funding of gain-of-function research — whatever the precise definitional debate — is a matter of legitimate public interest. The public has every right to a full accounting of how a pandemic that killed millions of people and reshaped the global economy began and what role, if any, US-funded research played.

The problem is not the question; it is the method. A Senate contempt proceeding conducted by a man who has already declared his desired outcome is not an investigation — it is a performance. The actual mechanism for establishing accountability would be a genuinely bipartisan independent commission with subpoena power, expert staffing, and a mandate to follow evidence wherever it leads. The 9/11 Commission model, for all its imperfections, produced findings that both parties accepted as broadly legitimate. That model has not been applied to Covid origins, and both parties bear responsibility for the failure: Republicans have preferred political theatre, Democrats have preferred to treat any origins inquiry as an attack on public health itself.

There is also a signal-to-noise problem. Every hour Rand Paul spends holding press conferences about Fauci's Fifth Amendment assertion is an hour not spent on the institutional reforms — to the NIH's grant oversight processes, to international biosafety standards, to pandemic preparedness infrastructure — that might actually reduce the risk of the next pandemic. The spectacle of a Senate contempt hearing has zero probability of producing useful public health policy. It has a very high probability of further poisoning the epistemic environment around Covid, vaccines, and public health institutions at precisely the moment when trust in those institutions has barely begun to recover.

History will not be kind to the decision-makers who allowed Covid accountability to become a casualty of partisan warfare. That includes the Democrats who reflexively circled the wagons around every pandemic policy regardless of its merits, and the Republicans who converted legitimate questions about institutional accountability into a vehicle for political revenge.

## What to watch

- **DOJ decision**: Attorney General Pam Bondi's Justice Department must decide whether to pursue the contempt referral. Given the legal complexity of Fauci's Fifth Amendment claim and the political costs of a high-profile prosecution, a decision not to proceed is plausible.
- **State charges**: The Biden pardon covers federal charges. State attorneys general — particularly in Republican-led states — retain independent authority. Watch for any state-level grand jury investigations.
- **Full Senate vote**: Paul bypassed the full Senate. Democrats will argue the committee-only vote is procedurally irregular. A legal challenge to the contempt process itself is possible.
- **Origins investigation**: Separately from the Fauci contempt, watch for whether the administration's intelligence community review of Covid origins — ordered by Trump's executive action early in the second term — produces any public findings.
