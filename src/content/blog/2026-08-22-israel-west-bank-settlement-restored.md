Israel has [re-established the closed West Bank settlement of Homesh](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cn7n0l4p0kzo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), defying growing international protests, according to BBC reporting on 22 August 2026. Homesh was one of four settlements evacuated by Ariel Sharon's government during the 2005 Gaza disengagement — a rare case of a settlement being dismantled. Its restoration represents a symbolic and legal reversal of that precedent. Separately, [BBC reporting notes](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c2lq5g4dedpo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) that Israel has been systematically expanding its settlement presence across the West Bank as part of a broader drive to reshape the territory. The UK, France, Germany, Italy, and Canada have [publicly condemned the move](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c998evlgz8ko?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), joining earlier condemnation from the UK, Canada, and Australia over [Israel's refusal to permit a criminal probe into aid worker killings in Gaza](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvgl2pe09eno?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## The received wisdom

The mainstream framing presents the Homesh re-establishment as a brazen act of annexationist politics by Israel's most right-wing coalition in history — a coalition sustained by settler movement figures like Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, who has openly described his goal as applying Israeli sovereignty across the West Bank. From this perspective, the move is not an isolated incident but part of a coherent ideological project: reversing the territorial ambiguity of Oslo, foreclosing the possibility of a Palestinian state by fragmenting the territory with settlements and bypass roads, and using the political chaos of the post-October 7 environment to accelerate changes that might otherwise face greater domestic resistance. The condemnation from traditional European allies and Canada is presented as evidence that Israel is now genuinely isolated even among its friends. The human cost is real: settlements displace Palestinian communities, fragment agricultural land, and are, under prevailing interpretations of international law, illegal under the Fourth Geneva Convention's prohibition on transferring civilian populations into occupied territory.

## A different read

The mainstream framing captures real dynamics. But it tends to underweight two things: the domestic political logic that makes settlement expansion almost structurally inevitable given Israel's coalition arithmetic, and the degree to which Western condemnation has become a ritual that neither side takes fully seriously.

On the first point: the Homesh re-establishment is not a strategic masterstroke but a payment of a political debt. Netanyahu's political survival depends on retaining the support of Religious Zionism and Otzma Yehudit, whose settlement constituencies regard any abandoned site as unfinished business. Homesh has been a particular obsession of the settler movement — yeshiva students have been returning illegally for years, and the 2023 Knesset voted to normalise their presence. Seen in this light, the formal re-establishment is less a new initiative than the belated legalisation of a fait accompli. This doesn't make it acceptable in international law, but it does explain why expecting Netanyahu to refuse the move is unrealistic given his current parliamentary arithmetic.

On Western condemnation: the joint statement from the UK, France, Germany, Italy, and Canada is the latest in a long sequence of such statements, each of which has been noted, filed, and disregarded by Jerusalem. The pattern is not new. Israeli settlements have been "condemned," "deplored," and "deeply concerned about" by Western governments for more than fifty years — and over that same period, the settler population has grown from a few thousand to roughly 700,000. This is not because Western governments are insincere in their displeasure, but because they have been unwilling to attach meaningful consequences to repeated violations of their stated position. The tools exist — suspension of trade preferences, restrictions on military exports, referral to international bodies — but using them would create domestic political costs and complicate strategic relationships that Western governments value more than they value the settlement question.

The deeper problem is that the two-state solution, which these condemnations implicitly defend, has been progressively undermined not only by Israeli settlement policy but by Palestinian political dysfunction. The Palestinian Authority governs parts of the West Bank in a state of endemic corruption and declining legitimacy, while Hamas controls Gaza under a governance model incompatible with any viable state. The condemnations assume a diplomatic framework — negotiations leading to a Palestinian state alongside Israel — that has essentially collapsed in practice. Restating the framework's requirements while the framework itself disintegrates is not policy; it is nostalgia.

None of this is an argument for complacency. The Homesh move is another step toward a one-state reality that neither Israeli nor Palestinian leadership has coherently planned for, and which carries serious risks for Israeli democracy as well as Palestinian rights. But the ritual condemnation without strategic consequence has demonstrably failed as a mechanism of influence. A more honest conversation in Western capitals would acknowledge that failure and consider whether different tools — including harder economic linkages — are worth their political costs.

## What to watch

- **Settlement legislation**: Whether the Knesset moves to extend Israeli civil law to Homesh and the other restored sites would represent a significant formal step toward de facto annexation.
- **US position**: The Trump administration's tolerance for Israeli settlement expansion has historically been significantly higher than European governments'. Whether Washington joins or distances itself from European condemnation will determine the diplomatic cost Jerusalem actually pays.
- **ICC jurisdiction**: The International Criminal Court has been examining settlement expansion as part of its broader Palestinian territories investigation. Whether arrest warrants for settlement-related offences materialise would be a significant escalation in legal pressure.
- **Palestinian Authority stability**: If the PA's governance capacity continues to erode, pressure will grow on Israel and the West to address the question of who actually administers West Bank Palestinians — a question settlement expansion makes steadily harder to answer.
