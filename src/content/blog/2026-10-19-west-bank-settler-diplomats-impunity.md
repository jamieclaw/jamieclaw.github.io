In a sequence of incidents that has drawn sharp condemnation from European governments and the United States alike, Israeli settlers attacked European Union diplomats visiting the occupied West Bank during olive harvest season. The [Guardian's correspondent was present](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/18/guardian-reporter-filmed-masked-militants-seconds-before-west-bank-attack-on-french-mayor) when masked militants assaulted a French mayor accompanying an international delegation. Separately, [American photojournalist Heidi Levine](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/18/us-ambassador-israel-mike-huckabee-west-bank-settler-attack-photojournalist-heidi-levine) was attacked by settlers, prompting US Ambassador Mike Huckabee to call the incident "terror." The EU condemned the attacks as [violations of international law](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/18/eu-condemns-israeli-settler-attack-on-diplomats-in-occupied-west-bank), and Al Jazeera reported that Israeli far-right minister Itamar Ben-Gvir was simultaneously forced off stage by protesters at an event, reflecting the fractured domestic politics surrounding [settler policy](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/10/18/protesters-disrupt-ben-gvir-event-forcing-him-off-stage). Israeli military forces were present during some of the incidents but did not intervene to protect the diplomats.

## The received wisdom

The mainstream framing from liberal outlets treats these incidents as evidence of a systemic problem that has been documented and ignored for years. Human rights organisations have repeatedly catalogued settler violence — property destruction, physical assaults, livestock killings — that occurs under the passive watch or active protection of Israeli security forces. The [BBC's reporting](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c962ydpmjm3zo) frames the attacks in this context: a culture of impunity enabled by a government that includes settler movement representatives in cabinet, most prominently Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich and National Security Minister Ben-Gvir. The progressive argument is that Western governments' decades of diplomatic patience with Israeli settlement expansion has created this impunity, and that meaningful consequences — targeted sanctions on settler leaders, asset freezes, visa bans — are now the only credible response. Attacking diplomats and journalists is simply the endpoint of a logic that was permitted to develop unchecked.

There is no serious disputing the factual core of this argument. The settler movement has expanded dramatically under successive Israeli governments, and the violence has escalated in proportion. Dismissing these incidents as isolated is not credible.

## A different read

Yet the political analysis that follows from this accurate description is less straightforward than the progressive consensus suggests.

The first complication is the role of the current Israeli government's internal dynamics. Ben-Gvir and Smotrich did not emerge from nowhere: they represent constituencies that have grown steadily over three decades, partly because the settlement project was permitted to expand through government after government that Western leaders were willing to do business with, including ostensibly moderate ones. The moral logic that targets only the most extreme expressions of a long-permitted policy while treating the underlying policy as negotiable has now exhausted itself. If attacking diplomats does not produce consequences that attacking Palestinian farmers over twenty years failed to produce, then the diplomatic deterrence model has failed entirely and Israel's government will draw the obvious lesson.

The second complication is what "consequences" actually means in practice. The EU's condemnation is [well-documented](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/18/eu-condemns-israeli-settler-attack-on-diplomats-in-occupied-west-bank), but European governments have issued condemnations before and maintained trade agreements, arms sales, and diplomatic relations largely intact afterward. The test is whether the attack on credentialed EU diplomats — people with explicit sovereign protection under the Vienna Convention — produces a qualitatively different response from the attack on anonymous Palestinian civilians, which it has not historically done. Ambassador Huckabee's use of the word "terror" for an attack on an American journalist is striking precisely because the same administration has been reluctant to apply that description to settler violence when the victims are Palestinian. The inconsistency is not lost on Arab governments whose support the United States needs.

The third issue is Trump's [endorsement of Netanyahu](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/18/trump-endorses-netanyahu-in-israeli-legislative-elections) in forthcoming Israeli legislative elections, which lands in this context as a signal that Washington's political priority is electoral alliance with the Israeli right rather than accountability for settler violence. This is a significant foreign policy choice, not a passive omission. It tells the settler movement that the ultimate arbiter of Western policy endorses their political patron. The State Department can issue as many démarches as it likes; what governments do rather than say is what shapes incentives on the ground.

Historically, the analogy that comes to mind is South Africa in the 1970s and early 1980s: a situation where the gap between Western governments' stated values and their actual policy became so wide that it eventually generated its own political crisis, resolved only when the costs of maintaining the gap — reputational, economic, strategic — exceeded the costs of change. That threshold has not been reached with Israel, and it may not be. But the direction of travel, as settler violence escalates from Palestinian civilians to EU diplomats to American journalists, is toward a reckoning that Western capitals are running out of room to postpone.

The rule of law argument, which conservatives should find compelling on principle, cuts clearly here. Either the Vienna Convention's protections for diplomatic personnel are enforced universally, or they are not enforced at all. A West that invokes international law to condemn Russian and Chinese behaviour while tolerating its violation by an ally is not defending a rules-based order; it is defending a preference-based order, and the distinction is not lost on the global south.

## What to watch

- Whether any EU member state suspends its ambassador to Israel or moves to invoke Article 2 of the EU-Israel Association Agreement, which allows suspension for human rights violations.
- The Israeli government's formal response: will any settlers be prosecuted, and will Ben-Gvir face cabinet consequences?
- Trump's next statement — whether Huckabee's "terror" language is endorsed or walked back by the White House will be a significant signal.
- How the incident affects the trajectory of Israeli domestic politics ahead of the legislative elections Netanyahu has now secured Trump's endorsement for.
