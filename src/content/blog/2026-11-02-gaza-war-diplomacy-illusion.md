Israeli strikes in Gaza have [killed eight people including a child](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmqln14rrzxvo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), according to hospitals in the territory, as the conflict continues into what is now its second year with no credible ceasefire framework on the table. Separately, Al Jazeera reported [thousands of Palestinians displaced from Hebron](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/11/1/thousands-displaced-from-hebr) as Israeli forces continued operations in the West Bank, extending the footprint of the conflict beyond Gaza itself. The civilian toll in Gaza has been a matter of profound international dispute since October 2023, but the basic facts of continued military operations, continued civilian displacement, and continued failure of sustained diplomatic engagement are not seriously contested. Hospitals in Gaza remain the primary source of casualty counts in the absence of independent verification mechanisms, a situation that itself reflects the broader breakdown of international access to the territory.

## The received wisdom

The dominant progressive framing of the Gaza conflict holds that Israel is conducting a disproportionate military campaign that constitutes collective punishment of a civilian population, that Western governments — particularly the United States and the United Kingdom — are morally compromised by their continued weapons supply to Israel, and that international institutions such as the International Court of Justice and International Criminal Court represent the legitimate path toward accountability. This framing positions Hamas's original attack as explicable by, though not excusable given, the conditions of the occupation, and argues that a political settlement requires addressing root causes — Palestinian statehood, an end to the blockade, and a negotiated framework for Gaza's governance. There is, within this account, a genuine moral seriousness that the right is too quick to dismiss. The death toll is real. The humanitarian conditions are catastrophic. The displacement of more than a million people is not a rounding error in a just-war calculus.

## A different read

The problem with the dominant framing is not its moral concern — it is its political naivety about what "a political settlement" actually requires and who has the capacity to deliver it. The progressive critique focuses almost entirely on Israeli conduct and American complicity while treating the architecture of Palestinian political representation as an afterthought. Hamas is not a government that negotiates ceasefires and then maintains them; its fundamental charter objective is the elimination of Israel, and its operational record — including the deliberate targeting of Israeli civilians on October 7, 2023 — is one of using Palestinian civilians as both shields and propaganda assets. This is not Israeli hasbara; it is documented by human rights organisations that are otherwise sharply critical of Israel.

The deeper difficulty is structural. Gaza has no legitimate government capable of post-conflict reconstruction. The Palestinian Authority, weakened by years of corruption and the 2007 Hamas coup, does not control the territory. Egypt has historically been unwilling to absorb Gaza's population or take responsibility for its governance. The Arab states that have normalised relations with Israel — UAE, Bahrain, Morocco — have shown no appetite for the political costs of demanding concrete Palestinian concessions from Israel in exchange for normalisation continuity. Iran funds Hamas and profits from the conflict's perpetuation. The incentive structure, in short, produces war.

[Israeli strikes](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmqln14rrzxvo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) killing civilians, including children, are a genuine moral catastrophe. But the international community's response — a cycle of UN Security Council resolutions vetoed by the United States, expressions of "deep concern" by European foreign ministers, and periodic calls for "immediate ceasefires" that neither party has any reason to accept — is not diplomacy. It is the performance of diplomacy by institutions that have lost the capacity to compel compliance from any of the parties who matter. A ceasefire that leaves Hamas in control of Gaza simply resets the conditions for the next October 7. A ceasefire that leaves Gaza in rubble without a viable reconstruction and governance plan produces a humanitarian emergency that radicalises the next generation. Neither the Israeli government nor the international community has articulated a coherent answer to the question of what comes after.

The harder argument — the one that gets conservatives accused of callousness — is that durable peace in Gaza requires Palestinian political reform as much as Israeli restraint, and that the international community's reluctance to make that demand honestly is itself a form of enabling. Treating Palestinians as passive victims of Israeli power rather than as political agents capable of choosing different leadership is not solidarity; it is condescension with a humanitarian veneer. The [displacement from Hebron](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/11/1/thousands-displaced-from-hebr) and the [strikes in Gaza](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmqln14rrzxvo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) will continue for as long as the political architecture that makes them rational — for both sides — remains unreformed.

## What to watch

- Whether the Biden administration's successor maintains or modifies the current posture on weapons supply to Israel — this is the single most important lever available to Washington.
- The status of any Qatar or Egypt-brokered talks; past rounds have produced temporary humanitarian pauses but no durable framework, and the conditions for a durable deal remain absent.
- Whether West Bank operations expand in ways that strain Jordan's capacity to absorb Palestinian refugee flows — Amman's stability is a critical variable in any regional scenario.
- ICJ proceedings and any European government decisions to impose conditions on arms exports — the legal and political pressures on Israel will intensify regardless of battlefield outcomes.
