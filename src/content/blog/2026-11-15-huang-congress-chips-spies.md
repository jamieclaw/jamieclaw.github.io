Former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has been [summoned to testify before Congress](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmq5vd1wdgz6o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) over his extramarital affair with a woman US intelligence reportedly believes was recruited by Beijing as an intelligence asset. Huang [abruptly resigned](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmze750zg0jeo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) last week after the affair became public, ending one of the most celebrated runs in Silicon Valley history. President Trump [confirmed](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqg7x7dpvym8o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) that the FBI has opened an investigation, saying he had spoken directly with Huang. Nvidia's board has retained a major US law firm to conduct an internal inquiry, and [interim CEO Colette Kress](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqgm37dv20e8o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) is managing the company while a permanent successor search begins. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is [facing intense pressure](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqvg2d1eg7zro?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) over allegations that his department knew of concerns about Nvidia's export compliance — and acted slowly. Nvidia's share price has fallen hundreds of billions of dollars in market capitalisation since the story broke, in [one of the steepest executive-scandal drawdowns in tech history](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6zxjx01vyleo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss).

## The received wisdom

The standard progressive and national-security-hawk framing converges here: a powerful CEO was compromised by a foreign intelligence service, the Trump administration's deregulatory instincts allowed lax oversight to persist, and the whole affair illustrates the dangers of letting Silicon Valley operate as a law unto itself on export controls. The Guardian's reporting [highlights the question](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/13/nvidia-scandal-sparks-us-national-security-concerns-china) of when senior Trump officials first learned about the alleged spy connection and whether Lutnick's Commerce Department was warned months before the public disclosure. That framing positions the scandal as a government-accountability story: who knew, when, and why didn't they act? Congress subpoenaing Huang is, on this reading, the system working as intended.

## A different read

The congressional-accountability story is real, but it risks obscuring a structurally more important problem that no Senate hearing will solve: the United States allowed a single company — one that became the world's most valuable at its peak — to become a critical chokepoint in the global semiconductor supply chain, then relied on ad hoc export-licensing decisions and personal relationships to manage the national-security dimensions of that dependency. That is not a Lutnick problem or a Huang problem. It is an institutional problem stretching back a decade.

Nvidia's dominance in AI accelerator chips — at one point holding an estimated 80% market share in high-end GPU compute — was [understood to have national-security implications](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c64g1gzy2vdzo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) well before this scandal. Huang himself told the BBC in a candid earlier interview that the chip race was inseparable from geopolitical competition with China. The export controls imposed under both the Biden and Trump administrations were attempts to slow Chinese AI development by restricting Nvidia's most advanced chips. But those controls were porous, contested by Nvidia's own lobbying, riddled with exceptions, and partially dependent on the CEO's own cooperation. Building a national-security architecture on a foundation of voluntary cooperation from the very executive you now suspect of compromise is not a strategy; it is a hope.

The larger irony is that the progressive critics now loudest about Lutnick's dereliction were among those who, three years ago, resisted the kind of aggressive export-control regime and domestic semiconductor industrial policy — embodied imperfectly in the CHIPS Act — that might have diversified America's dependency. Industrial policy is not glamorous; it requires government to pick winners, make bets, and accept inefficiency in the name of strategic resilience. The same technocratic optimists who trusted Huang's genius to solve every problem are now surprised that trusting one genius introduced single-point-of-failure risk.

The [Singapore dimension](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ck1exevgql4qo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) adds a further layer. That city-state, long celebrated as a model of pro-business governance and strategic ambiguity between Washington and Beijing, has found itself at the centre of allegations that Nvidia's semiconductor export controls were circumvented through Singaporean intermediaries. If confirmed, it would represent a serious blow to Singapore's claim that its neutrality is commercially neutral rather than strategically exploitable. It also suggests the actual enforcement problem is not at the Nvidia boardroom level but in the web of distributors, re-exporters, and special-purpose vehicles that the company itself may not have fully controlled.

Congress can subpoena Huang, hold dramatic hearings, and produce a blistering report. What it cannot do, without a fundamental rethinking of industrial strategy, is prevent the next version of this story.

## What to watch

Watch whether the congressional investigation reaches into the Commerce Department's internal communications from the period before Huang's resignation — that is where the most politically explosive material likely sits. Watch Nvidia's stock price as a real-time referendum on whether markets believe the company's operational continuity under Kress is credible; if it stabilises, the board's succession planning is working. Watch the [Anthropic IPO](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/crpdgd723x2zo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), which proceeds this week at a $1.3 trillion valuation — the market's willingness to absorb a major AI company debut in the middle of an AI-security scandal will reveal a great deal about investor time horizons. And watch whether China officially responds to the spy allegations, or maintains the studied silence that is usually more revealing than any denial.
